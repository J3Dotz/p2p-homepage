AI & Data Protection: AI Governance Assessment

Your staff are already using AI tools. This is how you find out what that means for the personal data you hold.

Why this comes up now

Most AI risk in SMEs isn't strategic. It's someone pasting a client list into a chatbot.

AI tools sit outside the systems you've already assessed. They're adopted by individuals, often on free accounts, and they process whatever gets typed into them — including personal data you're responsible for.

There's no separate AI compliance regime to satisfy in the UK yet. What you do have is existing data protection law applied to a new set of tools, plus a growing expectation that you can show you thought about it.

This assessment is about getting ahead of that: knowing what's in use, deciding what's acceptable, and writing it down before someone asks.

What the assessment covers

Four areas, and a written position you can hand to a client, an insurer or your board.

What's actually in use

A plain inventory of the AI tools your team has adopted, including the ones nobody signed off.

Data flows and transfers

What leaves your environment, where it goes, and whether the paperwork supports it.

Acceptable use position

A short policy staff will actually follow, rather than a ban that gets ignored.

Where a DPIA is needed

Which uses cross the threshold into a formal impact assessment, and which plainly don't.
